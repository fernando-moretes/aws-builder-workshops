Make your prediction before each command, and only then compare. That's where the learning happens.

**Fictional scenario:** 100 messages per second, 200 ms of service time, 6,000 messages in the backlog. [Open the calculator in the browser](/aws-builder-workshops/console/?lang=en&view=queue) or use the terminal.

<Steps>

1. **20 consumers: does the backlog drain?** Each consumer processes 5 per second.

   ```sh
   npx -y moretes-builder-workshops queue --arrivals 100 --service-ms 200 --consumers 20 --backlog 6000 --lang en
   ```

2. **30 consumers: how long does it take?**

   ```sh
   npx -y moretes-builder-workshops queue --arrivals 100 --service-ms 200 --consumers 30 --backlog 6000 --lang en
   ```

3. **What if the provider accepts only 80 per second?**

   ```sh
   npx -y moretes-builder-workshops queue --arrivals 100 --service-ms 200 --consumers 30 --backlog 6000 --dependency-limit 80 --lang en
   ```

</Steps>

## The answer key

Try to answer before you open it.

<details>
<summary>See results</summary>

| Scenario | Capacity | Utilization | Surplus | Drain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 consumers | 100/s | 100% | 0/s | never |
| 30 consumers | 150/s | 66.7% | 50/s | 120 s |
| 30, 80/s ceiling | 80/s | 125% | -20/s | never |

With 20, capacity equals the input: nothing is left to drain. With the 80/s ceiling, adding consumers doesn't create capacity at the provider, and the backlog grows by 20 messages per second.

</details>

## The math

```text
consumer capacity  = consumers × concurrency × 1000 ÷ service time (ms)
effective capacity = the lower of consumers and dependency
utilization        = arrivals ÷ effective capacity
drain time         = backlog ÷ (effective capacity − arrivals)
```

:::caution[What this math doesn't say]
It uses constant rates and ignores broker limits, batches, retries and failures. It's not a benchmark or a production setting. It's there to help you ask the right question before scaling.
:::
